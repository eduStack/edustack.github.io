Preschoolers, 3–6 years

Preschoolers start following rules in games with other children, tell stories, and begin to count and recognise letters.CDC · Milestones by 5 years ↗

What your child is developing

These are things most children can do by this age. They are for understanding your child, not a scorecard; every child develops at their own pace.CDC · Positive parenting tips: infants (0–1) ↗

Social and emotional

  • By 5, follows rules or takes turns when playing games with other children, and does simple chores like clearing the table.CDC · Milestones by 5 years ↗

Language

  • By 5, tells a story with at least two events and keeps a conversation going for more than three exchanges.CDC · Milestones by 5 years ↗
  • By 5, uses or recognises simple rhymes.CDC · Milestones by 5 years ↗

Thinking and learning

  • By 5, counts to 10 and uses words about time, like "yesterday" and "tomorrow".CDC · Milestones by 5 years ↗
  • By 5, pays attention for 5 to 10 minutes during activities like story time (screen time doesn't count).CDC · Milestones by 5 years ↗

Movement

  • By 5, hops on one foot and buttons some buttons.CDC · Milestones by 5 years ↗
When to talk to a doctor: If your child is not meeting the milestones for their age, has lost skills they once had, or you are worried about how they play, learn, speak, act or move, talk with your child's doctor. Don't wait: acting early can make a real difference.CDC · Concerned about your child's development? ↗

The research behind it

What each study found comes first, then the kind of study and where it was published. We prefer systematic reviews and meta-analyses, and check for new research every month.

  • In a study of 4- to 6-year-olds, the number of back-and-forth conversational turns with adults predicted activity in language areas of the brain better than the number of words children heard.

    Single-time studyRomeo et al., Psychological Science, 2018Study details

  • For children aged 1 to 8, play with gentle adult guidance did better than direct teaching on early maths, shapes and switching between tasks, and better than free play on spatial words. Other outcomes showed no difference.

    Meta-analysisSkene et al., Child Development, 2022Study details

  • A meta-analysis of 42 studies. More screen use was linked to weaker language skills, while co-viewing with an adult and better-quality programmes were linked to stronger skills.

    Meta-analysisMadigan et al., JAMA Pediatrics, 2020Study details

  • Following about 2,400 children, more screen time at 2 and 3 years was linked to poorer developmental screening results a year or more later.

    Long-term cohort studyMadigan et al., JAMA Pediatrics, 2019Study details

  • 9-month-old infants learned Mandarin speech sounds from live sessions with a person, but not from the same material shown on video or audio.

    ExperimentKuhl, Tsao & Liu, PNAS, 2003Study details

  • Meta-analyses of 75 studies found spanking was linked to a range of poor outcomes for children and was not linked to better behaviour.

    Meta-analysisGershoff & Grogan-Kaylor, Journal of Family Psychology, 2016Study details

Idea roots

Educators whose ideas connect to this stage. Open one to see what modern research supports and what it does not.
Friedrich Froebel 1782–1852 Froebel founded the kindergarten around play for exactly this age. Lev Vygotsky 1896–1934 Helping with just the hard part and then stepping back - "scaffolding" - builds on Vygotsky's ideas. Maria Montessori 1870–1952 Montessori preschools are among the best-studied ways of learning through self-chosen activity.

What you can do together

  • Keep reading together, and visit the library.CDC · Positive parenting tips: preschoolers (3–5) ↗
  • Let your child help with simple chores, and offer a few simple choices.CDC · Positive parenting tips: preschoolers (3–5) ↗
  • When they are upset, walk through the steps to solve the problem together.CDC · Positive parenting tips: preschoolers (3–5) ↗
  • Support imaginary play with props and stories, such as playing doctor.Harvard Center on the Developing Child · Activities for 3–5 years ↗
  • Encourage play with other children to learn sharing and friendship.CDC · Positive parenting tips: preschoolers (3–5) ↗
