Babies, 0–12 months

In the first year, babies form bonds of love and trust with the people who care for them, and learn by looking, listening, reaching and exploring.CDC · Positive parenting tips: infants (0–1) ↗

What your child is developing

These are things most children can do by this age. They are for understanding your child, not a scorecard; every child develops at their own pace.CDC · Positive parenting tips: infants (0–1) ↗

Social and emotional

  • By 6 months, knows familiar people and laughs.CDC · Milestones by 6 months ↗
  • By 1 year, plays games with you, like pat-a-cake.CDC · Milestones by 1 year ↗

Language

  • By 6 months, takes turns making sounds with you.CDC · Milestones by 6 months ↗
  • By 1 year, waves bye-bye, calls a parent "mama" or "dada", and understands "no".CDC · Milestones by 1 year ↗

Thinking and learning

  • By 6 months, puts things in their mouth to explore them and reaches for toys.CDC · Milestones by 6 months ↗
  • By 1 year, looks for things they see you hide, like a toy under a blanket.CDC · Milestones by 1 year ↗

Movement

  • By 6 months, rolls from tummy to back.CDC · Milestones by 6 months ↗
  • By 1 year, pulls up to stand and walks holding on to furniture.CDC · Milestones by 1 year ↗
When to talk to a doctor: If your child is not meeting the milestones for their age, has lost skills they once had, or you are worried about how they play, learn, speak, act or move, talk with your child's doctor. Don't wait: acting early can make a real difference.CDC · Concerned about your child's development? ↗

The research behind it

What each study found comes first, then the kind of study and where it was published. We prefer systematic reviews and meta-analyses, and check for new research every month.

  • 9-month-old infants learned Mandarin speech sounds from live sessions with a person, but not from the same material shown on video or audio.

    ExperimentKuhl, Tsao & Liu, PNAS, 2003Study details

  • Five-month-old babies looked longer when a screen appeared to pass through a hidden box, suggesting they knew the box still existed - months earlier than Piaget's account predicted.

    ExperimentBaillargeon, Spelke & Wasserman, Cognition, 1985Study details

  • Following about 2,400 children, more screen time at 2 and 3 years was linked to poorer developmental screening results a year or more later.

    Long-term cohort studyMadigan et al., JAMA Pediatrics, 2019Study details

  • A meta-analysis of 42 studies. More screen use was linked to weaker language skills, while co-viewing with an adult and better-quality programmes were linked to stronger skills.

    Meta-analysisMadigan et al., JAMA Pediatrics, 2020Study details

What you can do together

  • Talk to your baby, and answer their sounds by repeating them and adding words.CDC · Positive parenting tips: infants (0–1) ↗
  • Read and sing to your baby.CDC · Positive parenting tips: infants (0–1) ↗
  • Notice what your baby looks at or points to, and respond - "serve and return".Harvard Center on the Developing Child · Serve and return ↗
  • Play peekaboo and hide-a-toy games, which exercise memory and self-control.Harvard Center on the Developing Child · Activities for 6–18 months ↗
