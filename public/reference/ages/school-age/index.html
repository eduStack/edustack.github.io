School age, 6–12 years

School-age children's attention and language grow a lot, friends start to matter more, and learning to cope with failure becomes important.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗

What your child is developing

Thinking and learning

  • A 6-year-old can usually focus on a task for at least 15 minutes; by 9, for about an hour.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗
  • Around third grade, reading shifts from recognising words to understanding content.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗

Language

  • Sentences grow more complex; a 6-year-old can follow 3 instructions in a row, a 10-year-old about 5.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗

Social and emotional

  • Being accepted by friends becomes more important, and children may join in behaviours to be part of the group.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗
  • Children may "try on" lying or cheating as they learn how rules work; deal with it privately and calmly.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗

Feelings

  • Learning to handle failure and frustration without losing self-esteem is a key task.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗

Body

  • There are big differences in height, build and the age puberty starts.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗
When to talk to a doctor: If you suspect a learning difficulty, bullying, anxiety or low mood, talk with your child's teacher or doctor.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗

The research behind it

What each study found comes first, then the kind of study and where it was published. We prefer systematic reviews and meta-analyses, and check for new research every month.

  • Meta-analyses of 75 studies found spanking was linked to a range of poor outcomes for children and was not linked to better behaviour.

    Meta-analysisGershoff & Grogan-Kaylor, Journal of Family Psychology, 2016Study details

  • Across hundreds of studies, a growth mindset was only weakly linked to school results, and mindset programmes had small average effects. Students from low-income families or who were struggling at school may benefit more.

    Meta-analysisSisk et al., Psychological Science, 2018Study details

  • Randomised trials in Indian government schools found that teaching children at their actual learning level, rather than strictly by the grade syllabus, raised learning when it was implemented well.

    Randomised trialBanerjee et al., Journal of Economic Perspectives, 2017Study details

  • Using data on more than 350,000 adolescents, the study found that digital technology use explained at most 0.4% of the variation in well-being - a negative but very small association on average.

    Large dataset analysisOrben & Przybylski, Nature Human Behaviour, 2019Study details

  • A meta-analysis of 42 studies. More screen use was linked to weaker language skills, while co-viewing with an adult and better-quality programmes were linked to stronger skills.

    Meta-analysisMadigan et al., JAMA Pediatrics, 2020Study details

Idea roots

Educators whose ideas connect to this stage. Open one to see what modern research supports and what it does not.
Confucius 551–479 BCE (traditional dates) Children in one class can be at very different levels; teaching to each child's level, as Confucius did, is backed by trials today. John Dewey 1859–1952 Dewey's "learning by doing" fits this age, as long as adults guide it. Tao Xingzhi 1891–1946 Tao Xingzhi saw everyday life as education - chores and errands teach too. Jean Piaget 1896–1980 Piaget described this as the age of more logical, concrete thinking.

What you can do together

  • Play card and board games that need memory and strategy - from matching games for younger children to chess or Go for older ones.Harvard Center on the Developing Child · Activities for 7–12 years ↗
  • As children learn a game, step back and let them remember and enforce the rules themselves.Harvard Center on the Developing Child · Activities for 5–7 years ↗
  • Talk about what their friends are doing, so they can fit in without crossing your family's limits.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · School-age children development ↗
  • Aim for at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity a day, together where you can.World Health Organization, 2020
