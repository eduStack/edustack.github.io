Teens, 12–18 years

Teenagers move toward a more mature sense of who they are, separate from their parents, and turn more to friends - while their brains keep maturing into their 20s.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · Adolescent development ↗

What your child is developing

Thinking and learning

  • Early teens often think in black-and-white terms; by the middle teens, abstract thinking grows.American Academy of Pediatrics · Stages of adolescence ↗
  • The frontal lobes, which handle impulse control and complex decisions, keep maturing until well into the 20s.American Academy of Pediatrics · Stages of adolescence ↗

Identity

  • It is normal to start separating from parents and forming their own identity; this can bring conflict.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · Adolescent development ↗

Social and emotional

  • Friends become more important, and peer pressure may peak in the middle teens.American Academy of Pediatrics · Stages of adolescence ↗

Feelings

  • Body changes make many teens self-conscious and worried about how they compare with peers.MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine) · Adolescent development ↗
  • Pre-teens feel a growing need for privacy and may push back strongly against limits.American Academy of Pediatrics · Stages of adolescence ↗
When to talk to a doctor: Know the signs of anxiety and depression, and get help early if mood or behaviour changes last.World Health Organization

The research behind it

What each study found comes first, then the kind of study and where it was published. We prefer systematic reviews and meta-analyses, and check for new research every month.

Idea roots

Educators whose ideas connect to this stage. Open one to see what modern research supports and what it does not.
Erik Erikson 1902–1994 Erikson made identity the central task of adolescence; long-term studies show it forms gradually. Cai Yuanpei 1868–1940 Cai Yuanpei argued for balanced education - body, mind, morals and the arts - which matters as schoolwork grows. Jean Piaget 1896–1980 Piaget placed the start of abstract reasoning in adolescence.

What you can do together

  • Help your teen set a goal that matters to them, plan the steps, and check progress along the way.Harvard Center on the Developing Child · Activities for adolescents ↗
  • Encourage talking themselves through tricky situations to manage impulses.Harvard Center on the Developing Child · Activities for adolescents ↗
  • Support volunteering on a cause they care about.Harvard Center on the Developing Child · Activities for adolescents ↗
  • Agree screen and phone rules together, including protecting sleep.American Psychological Association, 2023
